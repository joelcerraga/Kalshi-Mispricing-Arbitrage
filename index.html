The research question

A discrepancy is only the starting point.

A sum of quoted prices can look inconsistent while transaction costs, missing depth or the contract’s settlement rules remove the apparent opportunity. The engine makes each of these assumptions explicit.

As Hasbrouck (2007) explains in Empirical market microstructure, the trading process matters for interpreting observed prices. As Wolfers and Zitzewitz (2006) discuss, reading prediction-market prices as probabilities also requires assumptions about the market and its participants.

01

Reconstruct the tradable quote

Convert complementary YES and NO bids into asks; retain fixed-point quantities; sweep the displayed depth and apply a stated fee model.

02

Audit the payoff states

Check event membership, threshold relationships and settlement contingencies before screening baskets or statewise portfolios.

03

Validate the probability model

Fit calibration on development dates, select on validation, then score the frozen choice on a later chronological sample.

Main findings / recorded sample

The apparent gaps did not survive the cost test.

Across 268 complete noon panels, 34 conditional historical basket checks had positive gross gaps. None remained positive under the stated current-fee scenario. Historical candles provide no executable depth or fill evidence.

The development calibration candidate also failed validation. The selected model retained the unchanged market-derived baseline; the later sample supplies no evidence of profitable model-based trading.

Scope of the evidence. The historical study covers daily maximum-temperature markets in New York, Chicago and Miami, 19 July–16 October 2026. Chronological splits are retrospective.
